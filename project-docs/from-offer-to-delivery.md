# From Offer to Delivery

A plain-English walk-through of what happens between a client and a professional, from the first offer to a finished, paid piece of work. Every stage below is live in the product today.

## 1. A project starts one of two ways

**Client leads — Direct hire**
The client finds a professional they like and sends them an offer — a price and a timeline — for a specific job.

**Professional leads — Proposal**
The client posts a job publicly. Professionals who want it send in their own proposed price and timeline.

Either way, the result is the same: one side is now waiting on the other to respond.

## 2. Either side can negotiate

Nobody has to accept the first number. Whoever receives an offer can send back a **counter-offer** — a different price, timeline, or a note explaining what they'd change. This can go back and forth as many rounds as needed before anyone commits to anything.

> System note: every counter-offer is saved, so there's a full record of who proposed what, and when.

## 3. Someone says yes

At any point, whoever is holding the current offer can settle it:

- **Accept**
- **Decline**
- **Counter again**

Accepting is the moment the deal is actually made.

## 4. The contract begins itself

Acceptance isn't just a status change — it immediately sets three things in motion:

- The job is marked filled.
- **Every other pending offer for that job is automatically declined** (so the client never accidentally double-books).
- Both sides are dropped into a shared project workspace where the actual work gets tracked.

## 5. The client breaks the work into milestones

Rather than one lump sum at the end, the client splits the total project value into up to five checkpoints — each with its own name, amount, and due date. Only one milestone is "active" for the professional at a time, in order.

> Example: "Frontend — ₹501, due Aug 23" → "Backend — ₹501, due Aug 27"

## 6. The professional submits proof of work

For the milestone that's currently active, the professional uploads what they finished — files, screenshots, documents — along with a short note describing the work. This is their formal request to be paid for that piece.

## 7. The client reviews and decides

The client sees exactly what was submitted — the note and every file — right next to the milestone, and either **approves** it or **requests a revision** with feedback.

Approving a milestone does two things at once: it creates a real payment record for that amount, and it automatically activates the next milestone in line for the professional. If a revision is requested instead, the loop goes back to step 6 for that same milestone.

## 8. The project completes itself

Once every milestone has been approved, the project is automatically marked complete — no separate "finish" step needed. Both sides can then leave a rating and review.

---

Every step above — the offers, the negotiation history, the milestones, the uploaded proof, the approvals — is also visible on a shared, real-time timeline both the client and the professional can check at any point, so nobody has to ask "what's the status?"
